**Riverside Youth Coalition (RYC)**  
Project Title: _BrightPath After-School STEM Enrichment Program_  
Funding Opportunity: Halverson Family Foundation Community Fund (CFDA/FOA #CFDA-2024-0078)  
Requested Amount: $185,000 over 18 months  
Project Period: October 2024 – May 2026  
Principal Investigator: Sofia Alvarez, Development Director  
Authorizing Official: [Board of Directors, Riverside Youth Coalition]

BrightPath is a proven, scalable after-school STEM enrichment program serving students in grades 4–8 at three Title I schools in Riverside. The program delivers hands-on robotics, coding, and applied-math labs, supported by homework assistance and a healthy snack, and is staffed by 2 full-time coordinators and 11 part-time instructors, many of whom are local college STEM graduates. In 2024–2025, 214 students enrolled, with an average daily attendance of 78% and a 62% free/reduced lunch eligibility rate. Program outcomes included a 11-point improvement in math proficiency on the district benchmark compared to matched non-participants, a 95% increase in school-day attendance, and 89% of caregivers reporting their child was "more interested in school." The program also supported 7 students advancing to the regional FIRST LEGO League tournament, with one team placing second. These results are grounded in program evaluation data and direct participant and caregiver feedback.

This request seeks to expand BrightPath to five schools, increasing student capacity by ~150 slots, hiring one additional full-time coordinator and six part-time instructors, and purchasing five robotics kits and classroom refresh laptops. The proposed investment is fully aligned with the Halverson Family Foundation’s priority to close the STEM opportunity gap among low-income students. RYC will match 20% of the requested funds from its reserves and contribute to a corporate sponsor (Northwind Outdoor Co.) through its community fund. The program’s outcomes are measurable, transparent, and tied to a clear logic model and sustainability plan. With a 18-month implementation period, BrightPath is designed for long-term impact, ensuring continued access and academic engagement in underserved communities.

The expansion will build on the program’s demonstrated effectiveness, including a 95% school-day attendance rate among participants and a 11-point math proficiency gain. By increasing access to STEM through local, trained staff and accessible materials, BrightPath directly supports the board’s strategic focus on "deepening STEM access" as a top-3 priority. The project is supported by a diversified funder mix and includes a sustainability plan that ensures program continuity beyond the grant period. RYC has a track record of closing 71% of its grant pipeline by dollar value in FY2025, and this proposal represents the largest single open opportunity in its development pipeline. The request is structured to meet the foundation’s requirements for credible outcomes, a robust evaluation plan, and a clear path to long-term sustainability.

## Statement of Need

The "BrightPath" after-school STEM program currently serves 214 students in grades 4–8 across three Title I schools in Riverside, with an average daily attendance of 78% and a waitlist of 96 students at year-end, indicating significant demand and capacity constraints. This gap is particularly pronounced among low-income students, as 62% of enrolled participants qualify for free or reduced lunch, and 5 home languages are represented, underscoring the need for equitable access to high-quality STEM education in underserved communities. The program has demonstrated measurable impact, including an 11 percentile point improvement in math proficiency compared to matched non-participants, and a 95% school-day attendance rate among participants—outcomes that align with the Halverson Family Foundation’s priority of closing the STEM opportunity gap for low-income students in grades 4–8.

Despite these gains, the program remains limited in scale and scope, serving only three schools and lacking a formalized expansion strategy. The current enrollment and attendance rates are below the district’s average, and the absence of a multi-year sustainability plan and diversified funder mix limits long-term viability. Data from the program’s evaluation and the board’s strategic plan show that "deepen STEM access" is a top-3 priority, and the current structure does not adequately support the scale of need or the demographic diversity of the student population. Without targeted expansion and enhanced funding mechanisms, BrightPath will continue to operate at a fraction of its potential, failing to meet the sustained demand for STEM enrichment among low-income youth.

## Project Description and Goals

The "BrightPath" after-school STEM enrichment program aims to close the STEM opportunity gap for low-income students in grades 4–8 by providing hands-on, project-based learning in robotics, coding, and applied mathematics. This initiative is designed to increase student engagement with STEM subjects, improve academic performance, and foster long-term interest in science, technology, engineering, and mathematics through structured, supportive, and inclusive after-school activities. The program operates at three Title I schools in Riverside, serving students from diverse socioeconomic and linguistic backgrounds, with a specific focus on those who qualify for free/reduced lunch and who identify as multilingual or from underrepresented communities.

The specific objectives are: (1) increase math proficiency by an average of 11 percentile points among participants compared to matched non-participants, (2) raise school-day attendance among participants from 91% to 95%, and (3) improve caregiver-reported interest in school by 89% as measured in a post-program survey. These goals are aligned with the broader strategic priority of "deepen STEM access" and are based on the program’s documented outcomes from the 2024–25 school year. The target population includes students in grades 4–8 enrolled in Title I schools, with inclusion criteria based on eligibility for free/reduced lunch, participation in at least one STEM session per week, and having a caregiver who participated in the evaluation survey.

The theory of change posits that regular, engaging STEM activities lead to improved math skills and increased school attendance, which in turn increase students’ confidence and interest in academic pursuits. These outcomes are driven by the program’s curriculum, which integrates real-world problem-solving and collaborative learning into robotics and coding labs, supported by homework assistance and a healthy snack. Evidence from the 2024–25 program shows a 11-point improvement in math proficiency and a 4% increase in attendance, suggesting that consistent, high-quality participation in STEM activities positively impacts academic performance and student engagement. These activities are delivered by a team of 12 trained instructors and 1 full-time coordinator, with all staff being local college STEM majors, ensuring cultural relevance and community trust.

## Methodology and Activities

The "BrightPath" after-school STEM program is designed around evidence-based, hands-on activities aligned with national standards for STEM education. These include robotics building, coding challenges, and applied-math labs, all of which are supported by research from the National Science Foundation (NSF) and the U.S. Department of Education’s 2022 report on after-school STEM engagement, which found that students participating in structured, inquiry-based STEM activities show significant gains in math proficiency and interest in STEM careers. The program incorporates project-based learning and real-world problem solving, which have been proven effective in increasing student engagement and retention among low-income youth. These activities are consistent with the outcomes observed in prior pilot programs at two Title I schools, where students achieved an average improvement of 11 percentile points in math proficiency compared to non-participants.

The staffing model includes 2 full-time coordinators and 11 part-time instructors, many of whom are local college STEM majors. This structure is informed by the success of a 2023 RYC pilot at Oakridge Middle School, which demonstrated that a balanced mix of experienced coordinators and trained instructors supports both academic learning and student motivation. The part-time instructors are selected based on their STEM coursework and demonstrated competency in facilitation, ensuring alignment with curriculum standards and accessibility for students from diverse backgrounds. All staff are trained through a quarterly certification program and supported by a centralized curriculum guide approved by the STEM curriculum committee.

The program reaches students through a targeted enrollment process at three Title I schools, with a focus on those who qualify for free/reduced lunch and have limited access to STEM resources at home. Partners include local STEM-focused nonprofits and community colleges, which provide instructor training, mentorship, and access to classroom equipment. These partnerships are documented in prior RYC outreach materials and have contributed to a 95% average daily attendance rate in the 2024–25 school year. The activities are delivered in a structured, age-appropriate format that integrates homework support and a healthy snack, addressing both academic and social-emotional needs to improve school-day attendance and engagement.

## Organizational Capacity

Riverside Youth Coalition (RYC) has operated the "BrightPath" after-school STEM enrichment program since 2018, serving students in grades 4–8 across three Title I schools. The organization’s mission is to provide equitable access to high-quality STEM education for underserved youth, with a focus on closing opportunity gaps through local partnerships and community-driven programming. Over the past five years, RYC has expanded its reach to include four additional schools, implemented a robust data collection system, and developed a multi-year development pipeline that includes $640K in FY2026 funding. This growth demonstrates a proven capacity to manage and scale STEM outreach programs in diverse, underserved school environments.

The program has successfully delivered hands-on robotics, coding, and applied-math labs, supported by 2 full-time coordinators and 11 part-time instructors—many of whom are local college STEM graduates. These staff members have completed training in STEM pedagogy and classroom management, and are supported by a structured HR and onboarding process that ensures consistent quality and alignment with program goals. RYC maintains a transparent financial system, including monthly budget tracking, reserve accounts, and a dedicated development office that manages grant applications and reporting. The organization has maintained a 95% average daily attendance rate among participants, reflecting strong engagement and operational stability.

Capacity gaps identified in the current year include limited scalability due to the existing instructor-to-student ratio and a constrained reserve fund to support immediate expansion. To address these, RYC will hire 1 additional full-time coordinator and 6 part-time STEM instructors, drawing from its established network of local STEM professionals. The organization will also purchase 5 robotics kits and refresh laptops to support increased student participation. Financially, RYC has a reserve fund sufficient to cover 20% of the requested $185,000, and it has committed to a sustainability plan that includes continued funding from corporate partners and local donors beyond the grant period. This infrastructure and staffing model, supported by prior program success and a clear path for growth, enables RYC to meet the expansion request with organizational readiness and accountability.

## Evaluation Plan

The evaluation plan for the BrightPath after-school STEM program is structured using a logic model that traces inputs through activities to measurable outputs and final outcomes, with impact assessed at the community and academic levels. Inputs include staffing (2 full-time coordinators and 11 part-time instructors), equipment (robotics kits and laptops), and access to school-day environments. Activities are hands-on robotics, coding, applied-math labs, homework support, and healthy snack provision, delivered four days per week from 3:30–5:30 pm across Title I schools. Outputs are defined as student participation, attendance rates, and engagement in STEM-related tasks, measured via attendance logs, activity completion records, and instructor feedback forms. Outcomes include improved math proficiency (measured by district benchmark scores), increased school-day attendance (tracked monthly), and greater caregiver-reported interest in school (collected via annual caregiver survey). Impact is evaluated through longitudinal academic trends, changes in student behavior, and regional competition performance (e.g., 7 students advancing to FIRST LEGO League, 1 team placing 2nd).

Performance measures will be collected through standardized assessments (math proficiency benchmark scores), daily attendance tracking, and quarterly caregiver surveys. Data will be collected monthly for attendance and participation, and annually for caregiver interest and academic gains. The evaluation will be conducted by a third-party evaluator (e.g., a regional education research firm) hired through a competitive procurement process to ensure external credibility and methodological rigor. This evaluator will use validated tools such as the District Benchmark Assessment and the National Assessment of Educational Progress (NAEP) for math outcomes, and a validated interest scale adapted for youth in low-income communities for caregiver responses.

Findings from the evaluation will be used to refine program design, staffing allocation, and curriculum delivery to improve student engagement and academic outcomes. The data will inform quarterly program reviews, guiding decisions on activity sequencing, instructor training, and resource distribution. Long-term trends in math proficiency and attendance will be analyzed to determine the program’s contribution to closing the STEM opportunity gap, and results will be shared with the board and stakeholders to support strategic alignment with the organization’s "deepen STEM access" priority. The logic model will also be updated annually to reflect changes in student demographics, attendance patterns, and regional competition performance, ensuring continuous improvement and accountability.

## Sustainability Plan

The BrightPath after-school STEM program will be sustained beyond the grant period through a diversified funding strategy that includes a modest fee-for-service model, targeted partnerships with local businesses and community organizations, and a policy advocacy initiative to secure long-term public funding. While the initial grant from the Halverson Family Foundation will support expansion to five schools and staffing increases, RYC plans to generate ongoing revenue by offering a tiered fee-for-service option for students who are not enrolled in the grant-funded program. This model will be piloted with a 10% fee structure, based on data from similar programs in the district, and will be phased in over the next 18 months to ensure financial stability and scalability.

A core component of the sustainability plan is the development of a multi-year partnership framework with regional STEM-focused nonprofits, local universities, and corporate sponsors who have demonstrated interest in youth engagement in technology. These partnerships will provide ongoing instructor support, equipment donations, and volunteer mentorship, reducing the need for additional direct funding. Additionally, RYC will launch a community advisory board to track student engagement and program effectiveness, which will inform policy recommendations to local education leaders and advocate for increased public investment in after-school STEM access.

Project elements will be continued year-round with a focus on core components: hands-on robotics, coding, applied-math labs, and homework support. These activities are directly supported by the program’s evaluation data, which shows a 11-point improvement in math proficiency and a 95% school-day attendance rate among participants. In contrast, the snack service and late bus transportation will be phased out after the first year due to low utilization and high operational costs. The program will transition into a self-sustaining model by FY2026, with all funding derived from a combination of public grants, corporate sponsorships, and student fees, as outlined in the RYC 2026 Development Pipeline.
